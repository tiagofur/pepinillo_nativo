# Impeccable — Design Guidelines for Pepinillo

## Design Context

### Users
Knowledge workers, students, remote workers, and creatives (LATAM-first) who do deep work daily. They open Pepinillo to **start focusing fast** — pick a task, pick a mode (Pomodoro / Flowtime / Time-Block / Stopwatch), go. Context of use: at a desk, on a phone lock screen widget, from an Apple Watch or Wear OS tile, or via Siri/Assistant. Every second of friction between "I want to focus" and "timer running" is a failure.

### Brand Personality
- **3 words:** calm, clear, native.
- **Voice:** premium but not solemn. The name "Pepinillo" is a playful contrast to the seriousness of deep work — the UI should feel trustworthy and focused, but never cold or corporate.
- **Emotional goal:** when the user opens the app, they should feel *"ok, time to work"* — calm and clear, but **NOT sleepy or meditative**. This is a productivity tool, not a meditation app. Energy is quiet, not absent.

### Aesthetic Direction
- **North star:** feel as if Apple built the iOS/watchOS app and Google built the Android/Wear OS app. **HIG-first on iOS, Material 3-first on Android.** No forced cross-platform look & feel — each platform uses its own idioms, components, typography, motion curves, and spacing.
- **iOS / watchOS:** SF Pro, SF Symbols, native NavigationStack, sheets, iOS 26 system glass aesthetic (`.glassEffect`, always-on, free — not a selectable theme), Dynamic Island for live sessions. Respect HIG spacing and hit targets.
- **Android / Wear OS:** Material 3 Expressive, Roboto/Google Sans, Material Symbols, Material You dynamic color from wallpaper, Glance widgets, rich ongoing notification for live sessions.
- **Themes:** light, dark, and follow-system — **user can pick any of the 3 explicitly** (already implemented).
- **Anti-references:** generic "Focus Timer Pro" Flutter-looking apps, over-gamified Forest-style visuals, meditation-app softness that puts the user to sleep, and any cross-platform framework aesthetic that makes both platforms look identical and foreign.

### Design Principles
1. **Native before unified.** When iOS and Android diverge, follow each platform's guidelines — never compromise nativeness for visual consistency across platforms. Functional parity is mandatory; visual parity is not.
2. **Calm, not sleepy.** Muted palettes, generous whitespace, restrained motion — but keep contrast, legibility, and a clear primary action on every screen. The user is here to work.
3. **One tap to focus.** The primary action (start a session) must always be reachable in one tap from the home screen, a widget, the watch, or a voice command. Design decisions that add steps between intent and timer-running are rejected.
4. **Quiet surfaces, loud primary action.** Backgrounds, chrome, and secondary elements recede. The current task, the timer, and the start/stop button are the only elements that earn visual weight.
5. **Motion with purpose.** Animations communicate state changes (session start, break transition, completion) — never decorate. Respect Reduce Motion on both platforms.
6. **Legible at a glance.** Widgets, Dynamic Island, ongoing notifications, and watch faces must communicate remaining time and current mode in under half a second, from across a room.
